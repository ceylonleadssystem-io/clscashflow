Ceylo, the setup checker

Ceylo is a small assistant inside the POS. It reads the business's configuration, checks it against what the business needs to trade, and says plainly what is in order and what still needs attention.

Status Concept · documentation only Owner Ceylonry product team Version 0.1 · 19 Oct 2026
Summary

What Ceylo does

Owners and managers set up the POS once and then rarely look at the settings again. Problems show up later, at the counter: a receipt that does not print, a kitchen screen that is not paired, a fifth cashier the plan does not allow. Ceylo runs a short health check on request (and quietly after a settings change), groups the results, and links each problem to the exact screen that fixes it.

Who uses itOwners and managers

Cashiers see a read-only "ask a manager" summary.

What it readsThis business's settings

Business profile, POS setup, printers, KDS, locations, users, plan and sync.

What it changesNothing on its own

Every fix opens the right settings screen; the person makes the change.

Character

Meet Ceylo

Ceylo's head uses the POS sidebar colour, its visor eyes use the Ceylonry orange, and its antenna is a tea leaf, a nod to Ceylon. The eyes carry its mood, so the same drawing works at 24 px on a button and at full size in the empty state.

How Ceylo talks

  • Short, plain sentences: "Your receipt printer is not set."
  • Says what will go wrong at the counter, then how to fix it.
  • Names screens the way the POS does: Settings › Receipt Printing.
  • Celebrates briefly when everything passes, then gets out of the way.

What Ceylo avoids

  • Technical terms such as stamps, documents, payloads or JWT.
  • Blame ("you forgot…") and alarm for small issues.
  • Guessing: if it cannot check something offline, it says so.
  • Showing secrets, keys, PINs or customer details.

Checks

What Ceylo checks

Each check reads settings the POS already stores. A Problem stops something from working; a Warning works today but is likely to cause trouble. Checks that do not apply (for example KDS for a retail shop) are skipped and not counted.

Flow

How a check runs

Start

Someone taps Ceylo or "Check my setup", or a settings save triggers a quiet re-check.

Read

Ceylo reads the settings already on the device. No extra download, so it works offline.

Check

Each check returns pass, warning, problem or skipped, with one sentence of reason.

Report

Problems first, then warnings. Passed checks are folded away.

Fix

The fix button opens the right screen. Ceylo re-checks that item when the person comes back.

After a quiet re-check Ceylo only speaks up if a new problem appears: the button gets an orange dot and a one-line notice. It never interrupts a sale in progress.

Rules

Ground rules

Read-only

Ceylo never changes a setting, sends a message or deletes anything. People make every change themselves.

Respects roles

Owners and managers see the full report. Cashiers see whether the till is ready and "ask a manager" for anything else.

No secrets

PINs, payment details, keys and customer data are never shown. A weak PIN is reported as "a user has a weak PIN", not the PIN itself.

Honest about limits

If a check needs the internet (sync, subscription) and the device is offline, Ceylo marks it "not checked" instead of guessing.

Light on the device

Checks run on data already loaded, in well under a second. Quiet re-checks wait until the screen is idle.

Accessible

Every status uses an icon and words as well as colour. The panel works with a keyboard and a screen reader, and buttons are at least 44 px on touch screens.

Scope

Not in the first version

  • Free-text questions answered by AI. The first version answers from the check results only; the cashier "why didn't it print" chat above is scripted from those results.
  • Fixing settings on the person's behalf.
  • A cross-account view in the admin portal. Later, the same checks could feed a "setup health" column on the accounts list.
  • Checks on the CashFlow app or Guest Manager.

Open questions

  • Should the quiet re-check also run once a day, or only after settings changes?
  • Is "5 days before payment due" the right warning window?
  • Should owners be able to mute a warning they accept (for example "no logo")?
